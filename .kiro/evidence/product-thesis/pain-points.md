## 1. Ranked Pain Points

### 1. Audit-prep panic and last-minute evidence scrambling — *(all: QM / EHS / Safety)*
- *(verbatim)* "Someone in leadership says 'we need ISO certification,' and then the entire organization panics for three months." — https://tallyfy.com/iso-audit/
- *(verbatim)* "Binders get assembled. Documents nobody has read get printed. People memorize answers to questions they think the auditor might ask." — https://tallyfy.com/iso-audit/
- **JTBD:** "When an audit approaches, keep me continuously audit-ready so evidence assembles itself instead of being reconstructed from scratch."

### 2. Documentation scattered across Word/Excel/shared drives with version chaos — *(all)*
- *(paraphrase)* Shared drives fill with "Procedure.docx," "Procedure_FINAL.docx," and "Procedure_FINAL_final_USE-THIS-ONE.docx," making it impossible to know which is current; obsolete copies linger and cause nonconformances. — https://msi-international.com/document-control/
- *(paraphrase)* Proof of a change ends up "scattered across a version table, a shared drive, an approval email, a downloaded PDF, and someone's memory of what was in force at the time." — https://slite.com/learn/document-version-control-for-compliance
- **JTBD:** "Give me one controlled source of truth so the current, approved version is unambiguous and old copies can't be used on the floor."

### 3. The certification-maintenance treadmill (recurring internal audits, management review, KPI collection, surveillance audits) — *(all)*
- *(verbatim)* Surveillance audits recur annually: "Year 1 – Surveillance Audit: USD 2,000 – USD 5,000" and again Year 2, plus recertification at year 3, on top of internal audits and documentation updates. — https://www.9001simplified.com/learn/iso-9001-certification-cost.php
- *(paraphrase)* Quality departments "work overtime just to maintain the required level of data," with customer data requests "triggering people working nights and weekends to generate reports and prepare for audits." — https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/99643-qualitys-dirty-secret
- **JTBD:** "Automate the recurring machinery — internal-audit scheduling, management-review inputs, KPI roll-up — so maintaining certification doesn't eat weekly hours."

### 4. The overstretched solo quality/safety manager wearing every hat — *(QM, Safety, IMS)*
- *(paraphrase)* At a small company the quality manager "ends up wearing a lot of hats… Everyone seems to think the quality/technical department is responsible for everything." — https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Quality_Manager/Reviews
- *(paraphrase)* A new/solo QM tasked with standing up a whole QMS is advised to just "start by documenting what you already do" because the scope overwhelms one person — Elsmar "QMS Overload" thread. — https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/qms-overload.89279/
- **JTBD:** "Let one person credibly run the whole system by cutting manual admin, not by hiring a team I don't have budget for."

### 5. eQMS/EHS software is enterprise-priced and unaffordable for SMBs — *(all)*
- *(verbatim)* "Traditionally, QMS systems have been targeted at enterprise level businesses – and they had price tags to match." Ranges cited: "$225 to $1000 per month for a startup," "an average of $2,500 per month" for growth-stage, "$7,500 or more for an enterprise." — https://www.isotracker.com/blog/how-much-does-qms-software-cost/
- *(verbatim)* "Full transparency is hard to find and too often the actual costs don't appear on providers' websites." — https://www.isotracker.com/blog/how-much-does-qms-software-cost/
- **JTBD:** "Give me transparent, small-team pricing that doesn't assume an enterprise seat count or budget."

### 6. Point modules/tools don't talk to each other — *(all, esp. IMS)*
- *(verbatim)* Quality Manager con: "it is not possible yet to have all these modules communicating with eachother." — https://www.capterra.com/p/153961/EQMS/reviews/
- *(verbatim)* QA-EH&S Manager con: "Connection with ERP system was difficult to implement"; another: "data extraction is not user friendly." — https://www.capterra.com/p/153961/EQMS/reviews/
- **JTBD:** "One connected system where document control, CAPA, training, and audits share data — not siloed modules I re-key between."

### 7. Adding a 2nd/3rd standard (9001 → 14001/45001) triples the work — *(EHS, Safety, IMS)*
- *(paraphrase)* Running the standards separately means "repeating the same activities three times: three sets of manuals, three audits, three management reviews and three risk registers," and 30–40% higher operating/audit cost. — https://www.4cpl.com/blog/why-iso-9001-iso-14001-iso-45001-together-reduce-40-compliance-cost/
- *(paraphrase)* A facility running separate 9001 and 14001 systems hit "duplicate documents, siloed responsibilities, and audit fatigue, spending more time managing the systems than actually improving performance." — https://www.qi-a.com/learning-center/integrating-iso-9001-14001-and-45001/
- **JTBD:** "Manage the ~60% of shared clauses once and attach standard-specific evidence, instead of maintaining parallel systems."

### 8. Incident/near-miss handling and CAPA follow-through fall through the cracks — *(Safety, EHS, QM)*
- *(verbatim)* "When near misses are reported but no action is taken, employees stop reporting. Lack of feedback signals that reporting doesn't matter." — https://www.safetyiq.com/blog/near-miss-reporting-how-to-turn-near-misses-into-prevention
- *(paraphrase)* Near-miss data only becomes a leading indicator "when corrective actions close on time and at scale"; in spreadsheets/email/paper, actions never reach the right owner. — https://www.ehsinsight.com/blog/6-step-near-miss-investigation-flow
- **JTBD:** "Route every incident/near-miss to a CAPA with an owner, due date, and closure evidence — with reminders so nothing quietly ages out."

### 9. Training/competence record chaos — *(all)*
- *(paraphrase)* Practitioners run competence in home-grown Excel matrices using "conditional formatting… to turn cells a color if dates exceed a TODAY() formulated cell," and a weak matrix earns a nonconformance because "the 'proof of competency' is how true your matrix is to reality." — https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/training-qualification-matrix-for-iso-9001-2015-audit.76064/
- Ongoing forum demand for how to prove training per clause 6.2.2 shows this is a chronic, unsolved chore. — https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/training-requirements-for-iso-9001-2015.69890/
- **JTBD:** "Track competence and training expiry automatically and produce audit-ready proof without a fragile spreadsheet."

### 10. Consultants are expensive and often leave behind an unmaintainable system — *(all)*
- *(verbatim)* "Consultant Fees (if applicable): USD 1,500 – USD 20,000+"; "Full-service consultancy starts at about USD 15,000." — https://www.9001simplified.com/learn/iso-9001-certification-cost.php
- *(verbatim)* Systems get "'built to pass' but not 'built to last,'" becoming "a mountain of unnecessary paperwork" that companies find "impossible to maintain." — https://iso9001group.com/consulting_diy_true_cost_iso_implementation/
- **JTBD:** "Get to certified and hand me a system I can actually run afterward, without a standing retainer."

### 11. Customer/tender pressure to get certified fast — *(QM, mgmt)*
- *(paraphrase)* The most common trigger is a company "waiting until ISO 9001 becomes a requirement for a contract they want to bid for," at which point the timeline is compressed and the system is built under pressure. — https://streamline.business/iso-9001-qms-for-tenders/
- *(paraphrase)* Realistic minimums are ~3–4 months even for a well-run small business, so a near-term tender deadline is a real squeeze. — https://www.aaa-cert.co.uk/post/how-long-does-iso-9001-certification-actually-take-for-a-construction-company
- **JTBD:** "Get me demonstrably certification-ready fast enough to answer a tender without faking a certificate that won't survive a client audit."

### 12. Over-documentation / building a bloated system that becomes its own burden — *(all, esp. first-timers & small firms)*
- *(paraphrase)* Over-documenting "is particularly common for small companies and those setting up the ISO system for the first time"; you're documenting "for your organization, not the registrar." — https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/iso-9001-for-a-really-small-business.31614/
- *(paraphrase)* Documentation "continues to accumulate over the years," leaving "outdated procedures, duplicated content, and unnecessary forms" that make the system inefficient. — https://dev.to/joy1/how-to-simplify-your-iso-45001-documents-58f9
- **JTBD:** "Keep the system lean — templated to the standard's real requirements — so it doesn't collapse under its own weight."

## 3. Recurring Pains NOT Hypothesized

1. **"Built to pass, not built to last" / documentation lags reality.** "The gap between the document and reality is where quality dies." — https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/99643-qualitys-dirty-secret ; https://iso9001group.com/consulting_diy_true_cost_iso_implementation/
2. **Weak reporting/analytics and data extraction inside the tools.** *(verbatim)* "The reporting and analytics tool has been a challenge to use," "data extraction is not user friendly," "The search function is very poor." — https://www.capterra.com/p/153961/EQMS/reviews/
3. **Org buy-in / quality seen as "the quality department's problem."** — https://www.effivity.com/blog/top-12-mistakes-in-iso-9001-implementation-and-how-to-fix-them ; https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Quality_Manager/Reviews
4. **Configuration/admin burden of the software itself.** *(verbatim)* "Takes time and some training to setup, administration and reviews are continuous." — https://www.capterra.com/p/101454/EHS-Management-Software/reviews/
5. **Registrar/auditor inconsistency driving defensive over-documentation.** — https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/iso-9001-for-a-really-small-business.31614/

## Sharpest single takeaway
SMB practitioners are **solo operators (pain 4) drowning in scattered documents (2) on a recurring compliance treadmill (3)**, priced out of enterprise eQMS (5) whose modules don't integrate anyway (6) — so they run it in spreadsheets where CAPAs and training expiries silently rot (8, 9), then **panic before each audit (1)**. Winning JTBD: "keep a lean, connected system continuously audit-ready with near-zero manual admin, at small-team pricing, across one or several standards."
